# 2026-08-12

## The licence, corrected

`D-036` chose PolyForm Noncommercial and it lasted a few hours. The flaw was one I had written down
myself and then not weighted: Noncommercial grants a **Distribution License**, and that covers the
source. I had noted it as "the private repository is what keeps the source in, not the text", which
is true and is not the same as the source being unlicensed. The Archmagos read it and said no.

The family table settles what the alternatives are. Strict and Noncommercial have the *same*
noncommercial scope — the same `Personal Uses` and `Noncommercial Organizations` sections, word for
word. Strict simply has no `Distribution License` and no `Changes and New Works License`. So it
solves the source completely, and takes the binaries with it, and builds were supposed to circulate.

Two answers that could not both be had from one unmodified text — and the way out is not to modify
the text. PolyForm's own README says that a modified PolyForm licence must remove every mention of
"PolyForm", which would cost exactly the recognisability that made a standard text worth choosing.
So the standard text stands untouched in `LICENSE`, and the one thing it does not cover is granted
beside it in `ADDITIONAL-PERMISSION.md`: an official build may be passed on, unchanged, free, with
both files, to someone whose use is also noncommercial.

The property that makes that legitimate rather than a licence edit with a different filename is that
it **adds and never subtracts**. A copyright holder may always grant more than a licence does; the
GPL's linking exception is the same shape. A test asserts the document says so in those words, and
counts both files into every archive — because an archive carrying the licence and not the
permission has silently withdrawn the one thing the permission is for.

Two smaller findings, both from checking rather than recalling. PolyForm **Strict is not in the SPDX
list** — only Noncommercial and Small Business are — so the manifests carry
`LicenseRef-PolyForm-Strict-1.0.0`; claiming an identifier that does not exist is worse than
admitting there is none. And the confidentiality half of "no source redistribution" is not a licence
clause at all: it is the private repository, plus a sentence in the README saying that access is
personal. Copyright and confidentiality are different mechanisms and only one of them lives in
`LICENSE`.

## Written for the project instead

`D-037` lasted about an hour. The two-file arrangement was correct and read badly: a standard
licence that forbids passing anything on, plus a separate page saying you may after all, is a thing
you have to hold two halves of in your head to know what you are allowed to do. The Archmagos said
he did not like it, and he is the one who has to hand it to people.

So the third answer is one file, written for this project. Worth recording that the standard texts
were not skipped — they were tried, in order, and each gave up something. PolyForm Noncommercial
licenses distributing the source, which was the one thing that had to stop. PolyForm Strict licenses
distributing nothing, which stops the builds too. And editing either costs the name that made a
standard text worth having in the first place, because PolyForm's own terms say so.

The cost of a bespoke licence was stated before it was chosen rather than after: nobody recognises
it, there is no SPDX identifier, and it has never been through a court or a legal department. That
is the trade, and it was the author's to make.

It is drafted in the PolyForm **shape** — short headed sections, plain words, second person — and
its first paragraph says so and disclaims being one of theirs, which is what their terms require of
anything derived from them. Kept from that shape: the patent licence, the patent defence and the
32-day cure, which are the three things a short licence usually forgets and which cost nothing to
carry. Added: the two clauses that are the whole reason this exists — the source is confidential and
access to it is personal, and a build may be passed on unchanged and free to someone also within
scope.

The test is not decoration. It asserts every heading is still present, because each one is somebody's
answer to a question I asked, and a licence loses a clause the same way any other text does: in an
edit that looked small. Proven by deleting *The Source Is Confidential* and watching it fail, and by
dropping `LICENSE` from one archive and watching the count come up short.

## A name

Can the copyright go under a pseudonym? Yes, and the reason is worth keeping straight: the notice
does not create the right. Authorship does. A notice is evidence and a courtesy, and Berne provides
for pseudonymous works by name.

Three things came with the answer, and all three were said before the change rather than after it.
It is **presentation, not anonymity** — `git log` carries the legal name on every commit and the
account is the link. **Enforcement needs that link**, and so does the "separate written agreement"
the licence demands for commercial use, because a contract is signed by a person or a company and
not by a handle. And a genuinely pseudonymous work has a **different term of protection** under
Berne art. 7(3) — 70 years from publication in the EU rather than life plus seventy — unless the
author is named beside the pseudonym or discloses later. Naming both would have avoided that; the
Archmagos chose the pseudonym alone, knowing it.

`Bunchyearth23`, in `LICENSE` and in the three manifests that name an author. The personal address
came out of the two that carried it, with nothing in its place, and there is still no contact line
for a commercial agreement — both offered, both declined.

The test holds the four files together by **agreement** rather than by listing what must be absent.
A test that spelled out the name being removed would have written it straight back into the tree it
was just taken out of, which is the kind of guard that defeats the thing it guards. It reads the
holder out of the copyright line and checks the manifests say the same, so it catches a drift to
*any* other name rather than a return to one.

The manual's cover still says `Soundnodes`. That is a document byline, not a rights notice, and it
was left alone on purpose — say the word if it should follow.
